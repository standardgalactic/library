\begin{abstract}

Human cognition rarely unfolds in isolation. It is almost always exercised within

teams, institutions, and other structured social environments that stabilize

communication, amplify agency, and enforce normative constraints. This essay

develops a mathematically rigorous framework for such environments---\emph{team

niches}---within the Relativistic Scalar--Vector Plenum (RSVP) field theory. In

RSVP, meaning, agency, and entropy are modeled as continuous fields over a

spacetime manifold. We show that a team niche can be treated as a structured

field configuration, and that transformations between niches can be captured by

a category \TNich{} whose morphisms are structure-preserving maps satisfying

three conditions: geometric coherence, trajectory preservation, and constraint

stability. We prove that \TNich{} admits colimits and supports a

functorial notion of \emph{institutional intelligence}, sending each niche to a

space of cognitive trajectories. We then sketch an $\infty$-categorical lift via

the nerve construction and a derived-moduli interpretation that connects this

framework to AKSZ/BV-style field theory. The core philosophical claim is that

human intelligence is not a property of isolated agents, but of dynamically

organized semantic niches; AGI, construed as a single machine matching ``human

cognitive abilities across any task'', thereby commits a category error.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Most contemporary definitions of ``artificial general intelligence'' (AGI)

picture a single machine that can match or exceed human performance across a

wide range of tasks. This picture treats cognition as a property of

\emph{individual} agents acting on \emph{discrete} tasks. Such an image is

seductive but misleading. Human intelligence, as actually exercised, is almost

never individualistic. It arises in families, teams, laboratories, courts,

clinics, companies, and states---in short, in \emph{institutional} and

\emph{team-structured} environments.

The central claim of this paper is that these environments are not secondary or

accidental. They are the \emph{primary} carriers of human-level cognition.

Individuals participate in them, and their cognition is shaped and amplified by

them, but the relevant object of study is the \emph{niche} itself, not a

stand-alone mind.

We develop this claim in the context of the Relativistic Scalar--Vector Plenum

(RSVP) field theory, in which cognition is modeled via three fields

\[

\PhiField,\quad \vField,\quad \SField,

\]

representing semantic potential, agency flow, and entropy density respectively.

Within this framework, agents correspond to localized perturbations or

trajectories in these fields. Team-level environments---what we call

\emph{semantic niches} or \emph{team niches}---are persistent field

configurations that stabilize communication, norms, and couplings between

agents.

Our aim is threefold:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]

 \item to formalize the notion of a team niche as a mathematical object in the

 RSVP manifold;

 \item to construct a category \TNich{} whose objects are such niches and whose

 morphisms are appropriately structure-preserving maps; and

 \item to show that institutional cognition can be captured as a functor out of

 this category, with a natural $\infty$-categorical enhancement and a derived

 moduli interpretation.

\end{enumerate}

The result is a unified picture in which:

\begin{itemize}

 \item communication becomes \emph{manifold alignment},

 \item teams and institutions become \emph{field configurations},

 \item institutional evolution is expressed as colimits in \TNich{},

 \item and the space of cognitive trajectories is functorial in institutional

 structure.

\end{itemize}

The conceptual consequence is that AGI, as typically defined, is not merely

ambitious; it is \emph{ontologically mis-specified}. Human-level intelligence is

not a property of an isolated agent acting on tasks, but of structured semantic

niches whose geometry, dynamics, and constraints cannot be captured by a simple

system.

\section{RSVP Background and Semantic Manifolds}

We briefly summarize the relevant aspects of the RSVP framework.

\subsection{The RSVP manifold and fields}

Let

\[

\M \subseteq \R^d \times \R

\]

be a spacetime manifold with coordinates $(x,t)$, where $x$ denotes spatial (or

more generally situational) degrees of freedom, and $t$ denotes time. The RSVP

fields are:

\[

\PhiField: \M \to \R,\quad

\vField: \M \to T\M,\quad

\SField: \M \to \R_{\ge 0}.

\]

Here $\PhiField$ is a scalar potential encoding semantic or meaning density,

$\vField$ is a vector field encoding directed agency or control flows, and

$\SField$ is an entropy density encoding uncertainty or multiplicity of possible

trajectories.

We do not need the full RSVP dynamics here; it suffices that these fields

reflect how meaning, agency, and uncertainty evolve and couple over $\M$.

\subsection{Agents as local semantic manifolds}

A single agent $i$ occupies a local region of $\M$ and maintains a \emph{semantic

manifold} of possible internal states. Let

\[

X_i(t) = (\Phi_i(t), \vField_i(t), \SField_i(t))

\]

denote the agent's local RSVP field state along its worldline. The space of

semantic configurations accessible to agent $i$ is modeled as a manifold

\[

\M_i^{\mathrm{sem}} \subseteq \Gamma\!\bigl(\M, \R \oplus T\M \oplus \R\bigr),

\]

where each point encodes a possible assignment of semantic potential, agency

direction, and entropy pattern consistent with the agent's capacities and

background.

\subsection{Propositions as submanifolds}

Within $\M_i^{\mathrm{sem}}$, a specific belief, intention, or plan is modeled

as a submanifold

\[

P_i \subseteq \M_i^{\mathrm{sem}},

\]

defined by RSVP-compatible constraints: particular semantic gradients, causal

assumptions, normative commitments, or task specifications. One can think of

$P_i$ as the set of internal semantic configurations in which a given

proposition or plan is regarded as true, rational, or actionable by agent $i$.

This geometric picture will underpin our treatment of communication and

team-level structure.

\section{Communication as Manifold Alignment}

Communication is commonly described as the transmission of symbols. In the RSVP

view, this is a degenerate shadow of a deeper geometric operation:

\emph{manifold alignment}.

\subsection{Communication between two agents}

Let $A$ and $B$ be two agents, with semantic manifolds

$\M_A^{\mathrm{sem}}$ and $\M_B^{\mathrm{sem}}$, and proposition submanifolds

$P_A$ and $P_B$.

When $A$ attempts to communicate a proposition to $B$, the underlying act is an

attempt to construct a structure-preserving map

\[

f: P_A \longrightarrow \M_B^{\mathrm{sem}}

\]

that lands in some region compatible with $P_B$ (or extends it). For this map to

be successful, it must respect several types of structure:

\begin{itemize}

 \item semantic coherence: alignment of $\PhiField$-related features;

 \item agency coherence: alignment of $\vField$-related directions (intended

 actions, goals);

 \item entropic coherence: compatible uncertainty profiles $\SField$;

 \item normative coherence: compatibility with $B$'s constraints and norms.

\end{itemize}

If no such map exists, the result is misunderstanding, confusion, or

non-communication.

\subsection{The role of shared structure}

In a high-dimensional semantic setting, two arbitrary submanifolds $P_A$ and

$P_B$ will almost never align. Generically, they are transversal or disjoint:

\[

P_A \pitchfork P_B.

\]

For a good map $f: P_A \to \M_B^{\mathrm{sem}}$ to exist, there must be

\emph{shared structure}:

\begin{itemize}

 \item common language and concepts;

 \item shared norms and expectations;

 \item similar training and background;

 \item overlapping tasks and goals;

 \item common physical or institutional context.

\end{itemize}

These structures reduce the effective dimensionality of the alignment problem

and stabilize the existence of good maps.

\subsection{From pairwise communication to team niches}

Once we consider not just a single pair $(A,B)$ but many agents communicating

repeatedly and under shared conditions, a new phenomenon appears: the emergence

of stable regions in which communication is reliably geometry-preserving. These

regions are what we call \emph{team niches}. They are not merely groups of

individuals; they are persistent configurations of semantic, agentic, and

normative fields that make manifold alignment routine instead of miraculous.

We now turn to the formalization of such niches.

\section{Team Niches as Field Configurations}

In this section we give a minimal but sufficient definition of a \emph{team

niche} as an object in a category.

\subsection{Basic data}

\begin{definition}[Team niche object]\label{def:nicheobject}

A \emph{team niche} $\mathcal{N}$ consists of:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]

 \item A smooth manifold $U$, the \emph{niche domain}, representing the

 region of the RSVP manifold where the niche is active.

 \item A set $A$ of agents, together with trajectories

 \[

 X_i: [0,T_i] \to U,\quad i \in A,

 \]

 of class $C^1$ or higher.

 \item A set $B$ of \emph{constraints} or \emph{boundary conditions}, encoding

 institutional rules, safety limits, thermodynamic bounds, or semantic norms

 that must hold within the niche.

\end{enumerate}

We write $\mathcal{N} = (U,A,B)$, omitting the trajectories from the notation

when context permits.

\end{definition}

Intuitively:

\begin{itemize}

 \item $U$ encodes the physical or conceptual arena in which the team operates

 (a lab, a court, a firm, a technical domain).

 \item $A$ encodes the individuals participating in the niche.

 \item The trajectories $X_i$ encode how these individuals move through the

 niche domain over time---physically, cognitively, or both.

 \item $B$ encodes the constraints that shape and stabilize these trajectories.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Constraints as admissible configurations}

We treat $B$ abstractly as a set of conditions that trajectories and fields in

$U$ must satisfy. In many concrete cases, one may regard $B$ as a subset of a

larger configuration space:

\[

B \subseteq \mathcal{C}(U),

\]

where $\mathcal{C}(U)$ might include RSVP field configurations, resource

allocations, or admissible communication patterns. For our categorical

constructions, it is enough that $B$ is partially ordered by inclusion or

refinement.

\section{Morphisms of Team Niches}

We now define the morphisms between niches. The key idea is that a morphism

should encode a transformation that preserves \emph{structure} in three senses:

geometric, dynamical, and normative.

\subsection{Morphisms as structure-preserving transformations}

\begin{definition}[Structure-preserving morphism]\label{def:morphism}

Let

\[

\mathcal{N} = (U,A,B), \qquad

\mathcal{N}' = (U',A',B')

\]

be team niches. A \emph{morphism} $f:\mathcal{N} \to \mathcal{N}'$ consists of

a triple

\[

f = (f_U,f_A,f_B)

\]

where:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]

 \item $f_U:U \to U'$ is a $C^k$ map for some fixed $k\ge 1$;

 \item $f_A:A \to A'$ is a map of agent sets;

 \item $f_B:B \to B'$ is a monotone map of constraints;

\end{enumerate}

such that the following conditions hold:

\begin{description}

 \item[\textnormal{(G) Geometric coherence.}]

 The differential $Df_U$ is bounded in $C^{k-1}$-norm:

 \[

 \|Df_U\|_{C^{k-1}(U)} \le C_G

 \]

 for some constant $C_G$, and for all $x,y\in U$ we have

 \[

 d_{U'}\bigl(f_U(x),f_U(y)\bigr) \le (1+\dist(f))\, d_U(x,y),

 \]

 where $d_U$ and $d_{U'}$ are chosen Riemannian metrics and $\dist(f)$ is a

 nonnegative real number called the \emph{distortion modulus} of $f$.

 \item[\textnormal{(T) Trajectory preservation.}]

 For each $i\in A$ with trajectory $X_i:[0,T_i]\to U$, there exists a

 (reparametrized) trajectory $X_{f_A(i)}':[0,T_i']\to U'$ such that

 \[

 \bigl\| X_{f_A(i)}' - f_U \circ X_i \bigr\|_{C^1([0,T_i])} \le \dist(f).

 \]

 \item[\textnormal{(C) Constraint stability.}]

 For all $b\in B$, the image $f_B(b)$ is in $B'$ and represents a constraint at least

 as strict as $b$ under the transformation $f_U$; in particular, any admissible

 configuration in $B$ remains admissible (or becomes more constrained) when

 pushed forward along $f$.

\end{description}

\end{definition}

Condition (G) ensures that $f_U$ does not violently distort the niche geometry;

condition (T) ensures that individual agents' trajectories are preserved up to

small error; condition (C) ensures that institutional, ethical, or physical

constraints are not weakened by the transformation.

\subsection{The distortion modulus and morphism topology}

\begin{definition}[Distortion modulus]\label{def:modulus}

Given a morphism $f=(f_U,f_A,f_B)$, its \emph{distortion modulus} $\dist(f)$ is

defined as

\[

\dist(f) := \max\Bigl\{

\|Df_U - \mathrm{id}\|_{C^{k-1}(U)},

\ \sup_{i\in A}\,\| X_{f_A(i)}' - f_U\circ X_i\|_{C^1([0,T_i])},

\ \Delta_B(f)

\Bigr\},

\]

where $\Delta_B(f)$ measures the maximal ``distance'' by which constraints are

tightened in a suitable metric on the space of constraints.

\end{definition}

One can equip each hom-set $\Hom(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N}')$ with a topology in

which a sequence $f_n$ converges to $f$ if and only if

$\dist(f_n-f)\to 0$. This turns hom-sets into topological spaces and prepares

the ground for enrichment and $\infty$-categorical lifting.

\begin{remark}

The modulus $\dist(f)$ serves two roles: it quantifies geometric and dynamical

deviation from identity, and it encodes semantic or normative drift. This is

crucial for modeling real institutions, which never transform perfectly

rigidly.

\end{remark}

\section{The Category \texorpdfstring{\TNich}{TNich}}

We now verify that team niches and structure-preserving morphisms form a

category.

\subsection{Composition and identities}

\begin{definition}[Category \TNich]\label{def:category}

The category \TNich{} is defined as follows:

\begin{itemize}

 \item Objects are team niches $\mathcal{N}=(U,A,B)$.

 \item Morphisms are structure-preserving maps $f:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}'$

 as in \Cref{def:morphism}.

\end{itemize}

\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Identities]\label{prop:identity}

For every niche $\mathcal{N}=(U,A,B)$, the triple

\[

\id_{\mathcal{N}} := (\id_U,\id_A,\id_B)

\]

is a morphism $\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}$ with distortion modulus $\dist(\id_{\mathcal{N}})=0$.

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}

Trivially, $\id_U$ is a $C^k$ map with derivative equal to the identity, so

$\|D\id_U - \mathrm{id}\|=0$. For each $i\in A$,

\[

X_{i}' = \id_U\circ X_i = X_i,

\]

so $\|X_i' - X_i\|_{C^1}=0$. Constraints are preserved exactly under $\id_B$.

Thus (G), (T), and (C) hold with $\dist=0$.

\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Composition]\label{prop:composition}

If $f:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}'$ and $g:\mathcal{N}'\to\mathcal{N}''$ are

morphisms, then

\[

g\circ f := (g_U\circ f_U,\ g_A\circ f_A,\ g_B\circ f_B)

\]

is a morphism $\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}''$.

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}

Geometric coherence follows from the chain rule and the subadditivity of

distortion. For any $x,y\in U$,

\[

d_{U''}\bigl(g_U(f_U(x)),g_U(f_U(y))\bigr)

\le (1+\dist(g))\, d_{U'}\bigl(f_U(x),f_U(y)\bigr)

\le (1+\dist(g))(1+\dist(f))\, d_U(x,y).

\]

Thus $\dist(g\circ f)\le \dist(g)+\dist(f)$ up to lower-order terms.

For trajectories, given $i\in A$,

\[

\bigl\| X_{g_A(f_A(i))}'' - g_U(f_U(X_i))\bigr\|

\le

\bigl\| X_{g_A(f_A(i))}'' - g_U(X_{f_A(i)}')\bigr\|

+ \bigl\|g_U(X_{f_A(i)}') - g_U(f_U(X_i))\bigr\|

\]

and each term is bounded by the respective distortion modulus.

Constraint stability is preserved because $f_B(B)\subseteq B'$ and

$g_B(B')\subseteq B''$ imply $(g_B\circ f_B)(B)\subseteq B''$.

\end{proof}

Associativity of composition and the left/right identity laws follow directly

from associativity and identity laws of function composition, giving:

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:category}

\TNich{} is a well-defined category.

\end{theorem}

\section{Limits, Colimits, and Niche Evolution}

One of the main motivations for constructing \TNich{} is that institutional

evolution, mergers, and federations can be captured as limits and colimits in

this category.

\subsection{Products and parallel composition}

For independent niches, we can form a categorical product that models parallel

teams.

\begin{proposition}[Product of independent niches]

Let $\mathcal{N}_1=(U_1,A_1,B_1)$ and $\mathcal{N}_2=(U_2,A_2,B_2)$ be niches

with disjoint domains and independent constraints. Their product is the niche

\[

\mathcal{N}_1 \times \mathcal{N}_2 :=

(U_1 \sqcup U_2,\ A_1 \sqcup A_2,\ B_1 \sqcup B_2),

\]

equipped with projection morphisms satisfying the universal property of the

product in the subcategory of independent niches.

\end{proposition}

\subsection{Coproducts and non-interacting unions}

Dually, one can define coproducts as disjoint unions of niches when no coupling

is yet imposed between them. This models institutions that coexist without

integration.

\subsection{Pushouts and mergers}

More interesting is the case where two niches share a common sub-niche and

become integrated.

\begin{theorem}[Pushouts as niche mergers]\label{thm:pushout}

Suppose we have niches $\mathcal{N}_0,\mathcal{N}_1,\mathcal{N}_2$ and

structure-preserving embeddings

\[

f_1:\mathcal{N}_0 \to \mathcal{N}_1,\qquad

f_2:\mathcal{N}_0 \to \mathcal{N}_2.

\]

Then there exists a niche $\mathcal{P}$ and morphisms

\[

i_1:\mathcal{N}_1 \to \mathcal{P},\qquad

i_2:\mathcal{N}_2 \to \mathcal{P}

\]

such that the square

\[

\begin{tikzcd}

\mathcal{N}_0 \arrow[r,"f_1"] \arrow[d,"f_2"']

 & \mathcal{N}_1 \arrow[d,"i_1"] \\

\mathcal{N}_2 \arrow[r,"i_2"']

 & \mathcal{P}

\end{tikzcd}

\]

is a pushout in \TNich{}: for any niche $\mathcal{M}$ and morphisms

$g_1:\mathcal{N}_1\to\mathcal{M}$, $g_2:\mathcal{N}_2\to\mathcal{M}$ with

$g_1\circ f_1 = g_2\circ f_2$, there exists a unique morphism

$h:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{M}$ such that $h\circ i_1 = g_1$ and

$h\circ i_2 = g_2$.

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Idea of proof]

At the level of domains, form the pushout manifold $U_1 \sqcup_{U_0} U_2$

(gluing along the images of $f_{1,U}$ and $f_{2,U}$). At the level of agents,

form the coequalizer of $A_1 \sqcup A_2$ under the identifications induced by

$f_{1,A}$ and $f_{2,A}$. At the level of constraints, take the colimit of $B_0

\to B_1$ and $B_0\to B_2$ in the poset of constraints. One then checks that the

resulting niche $\mathcal{P}$ satisfies the universal property in the sense of

structure-preserving morphisms.

\end{proof}

This construction models a wide range of real institutional operations: mergers

of departments, formation of joint research centers, or integration of formerly

separate regulatory bodies.

\subsection{Directed colimits and institutional evolution}

More generally, a directed system of niches

\[

\mathcal{N}_0 \to \mathcal{N}_1 \to \mathcal{N}_2 \to \cdots

\]

can be understood as a sequence of refinements, expansions, or reorganizations

of an institution. The colimit

\[

\colim \mathcal{N}_i

\]

then represents the \emph{evolutionary limit niche}: the structure that emerges

in the long run. Under suitable regularity assumptions, such colimits exist in

\TNich{} and have the expected universal property.

\section{Institutional Intelligence as a Functor}

We now formalize the idea that institutions carry \emph{cognitive trajectories}

in a functorial way.

\subsection{The category of cognitive trajectories}

Let \CTraj{} denote a category whose objects are families of trajectories

$\{X_i\}_{i\in A}$ on a domain $U$ subject to constraints, and whose morphisms

are structure-preserving transformations of such families (for example, reindexing

agents, reparametrizing time, or embedding trajectories into larger spaces).

We keep \CTraj{} abstract, since different applications will require different

structures; the crucial point is that trajectories can be treated as objects in

a category.

\subsection{The trajectory functor}

\begin{definition}[Trajectory functor]\label{def:trajfunctor}

Define a functor

\[

F:\TNich \longrightarrow \CTraj

\]

by:

\begin{itemize}

 \item On objects: $F(U,A,B)$ is the family of trajectories

 $\{X_i:[0,T_i]\to U\}_{i\in A}$ subject to $B$.

 \item On morphisms: for $f:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}'$,

 $F(f)$ maps each trajectory $X_i$ to the trajectory $X_{f_A(i)}'$ that

 approximates $f_U\circ X_i$ within the distortion modulus $\dist(f)$.

\end{itemize}

\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Functoriality of institutional intelligence]\label{thm:functor}

The assignment $F:\TNich\to\CTraj$ of \Cref{def:trajfunctor} is a well-defined

functor: it preserves identity morphisms and composition.

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}

For an identity $\id_\mathcal{N}$, $F(\id_\mathcal{N})$ sends each $X_i$ to

itself, so $F(\id_\mathcal{N})=\id_{F(\mathcal{N})}$. For composition, if

$g\circ f$ is defined, the associated trajectory map $F(g\circ f)$ coincides

with $F(g)\circ F(f)$ up to the composition of the respective distortions.

Since distortions are bounded and additive, this induces the same morphism in

\CTraj{}. Thus $F$ is functorial.

\end{proof}

\begin{remark}

This result justifies speaking of \emph{institutional intelligence} as a

functorial object: institutional transformations induce coherent transformations

of the cognitive trajectories they support.

\end{remark}

\section{Enrichment, Nerves, and $\infty$-Categorical Lift}

The topology on hom-sets given by the distortion modulus suggests that \TNich{}

is naturally enriched in topological spaces or simplicial sets. This in turn

enables an $\infty$-categorical enhancement.

\subsection{Topological enrichment}

Equipping each $\Hom(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N}')$ with the topology induced by

$\dist$ yields a topologically enriched category: morphisms form spaces, not

just sets, and continuous deformations of morphisms can encode gradual

institutional change.

\subsection{The nerve and higher morphisms}

Taking the simplicial nerve $N(\TNich)$ yields a simplicial set whose:

\begin{itemize}

 \item $0$-simplices are niches,

 \item $1$-simplices are morphisms,

 \item $2$-simplices encode commutative triangles of institutional

 transformations,

 \item higher simplices encode higher coherence data.

\end{itemize}

Under reasonable fibrancy assumptions, $N(\TNich)$ can be regarded as an

$(\infty,1)$-category $\mathcal{T}$, in which:

\begin{itemize}

 \item $1$-morphisms are niche transformations,

 \item $2$-morphisms are homotopies of such transformations (for example,

 families of policy updates),

 \item higher morphisms capture coherence between multi-step institutional

 reforms.

\end{itemize}

This $\infty$-categorical structure aligns naturally with existing frameworks

for homotopy-coherent systems in higher category theory.

\section{Derived Geometry and Moduli Interpretation}

We briefly sketch how team niches can be embedded in a derived-geometric

framework reminiscent of moduli stacks in field theory.

\subsection{Niches as points in a moduli stack}

Given a fixed RSVP background field $(\PhiField,\vField,\SField)$, a team niche

$\mathcal{N}=(U,A,B)$ can be seen as a point in a moduli space of admissible

field configurations with marked trajectories and constraints. One can define a

stack $\mathfrak{N}$ whose:

\begin{itemize}

 \item objects are triples $(U,A,B)$ plus RSVP-compatible structures,

 \item morphisms are structure-preserving maps as in \Cref{def:morphism}.

\end{itemize}

This stack can be endowed with additional structure (for example, shifted

symplectic forms) following techniques from derived algebraic geometry.

\subsection{Analogy with AKSZ/BV}

In AKSZ-type topological field theories, a sigma model is defined by mapping a

source manifold into a target space with additional structure; BV formalism

treats solution spaces as derived critical loci equipped with symplectic forms.

In our setting:

\begin{itemize}

 \item the niche domain $U$ plays the role of a worldvolume,

 \item RSVP fields form the target,

 \item trajectories correspond to field configurations,

 \item constraints $B$ play the role of boundary conditions or gauge-fixing

 conditions.

\end{itemize}

Morphisms of niches resemble solution-preserving maps of field theories; the

distortion modulus measures deviation from exact solution-preservation.

This parallel is not exact (RSVP is not topological), but the pattern of

\emph{maps between structured field configurations preserving solutions up to

controlled deformation} is shared.

\section{Philosophical and Ethical Remarks}

The formalism developed here has conceptual and ethical implications.

\subsection{Cognition as a property of niches}

If cognition is modeled by trajectories in RSVP fields, and these trajectories

are stabilized, corrected, and amplified by team niches, then human intelligence

is not a property of isolated individuals. It is a property of

\emph{institutionally embedded} agents. Our category \TNich{} makes this precise

by treating niches as the primary objects and individual trajectories as

functorial images.

\subsection{AGI as category error}

Standard AGI definitions posit a single system capable of performing any human

task. In our framework, tasks are not free-standing objects; they are

\emph{trajectories in specific niches}. The notion of matching human

performance across tasks'' is under-specified if one does not also specify the

niches in which those tasks are embedded. A machine that does not inhabit a

team niche in the RSVP sense cannot meaningfully be said to share human-level

cognitive abilities''; it lacks the relevant object in the category.

\subsection{Constraint monotonicity and ethics}

Constraint monotonicity---the requirement that morphisms do not weaken

constraints---provides a formal analog of ethical preservation: institutional

transformations should not introduce new violations of safety or justice. While

this is a toy idealization, it suggests a direction for formalizing normative

requirements on institutional change.

\section{Examples}

We conclude with brief illustrations of how niches and morphisms model familiar

institutional scenarios.

\begin{example}[Research lab]

Let $U$ be the physical and conceptual space of a laboratory, with agents $A$

the researchers and students, and constraints $B$ including safety protocols,

publication practices, and funding rules. Trajectories $X_i$ capture not only

physical movement but also the evolution of research projects. A morphism

$f:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}'$ could model the lab's integration into a larger

institute, with $f_U$ embedding $U$ into a bigger campus, $f_A$ reindexing

researchers, and $f_B$ adding new grant and review constraints.

\end{example}

\begin{example}[Surgical team]

Here $U$ is the operating room and perioperative environment, $A$ is the set of

surgeons, anesthesiologists, nurses, and technicians, and $B$ is the set of

clinical protocols and ethical constraints. Trajectories encode roles over the

course of procedures. A morphism could model the adoption of a new surgical

technique that preserves role structure and safety constraints while changing

certain trajectories.

\end{example}

\begin{example}[Court system]

$U$ is the institutional space of a court, $A$ includes judges, lawyers, and

staff, and $B$ is the set of legal and procedural rules. A morphism could map a

local court niche into a higher court system, embedding cases and procedures

into a broader appellate structure.

\end{example}

These examples are schematic, but they show how the formalism can be applied to

real institutional settings.

\section{Conclusion}

We have introduced a category \TNich{} of team niches as structured field

configurations in the RSVP framework, defined structure-preserving morphisms

between them, and shown that institutional intelligence is a functor from

\TNich{} to a category of cognitive trajectories. We have indicated how \TNich{}

admits a natural topological enrichment and an $\infty$-categorical lift, and

briefly sketched a derived moduli interpretation.

Beyond its technical content, this framework reframes human cognition as a

property of semantic niches rather than isolated agents. On this view, AGI

defined as a single machine matching human task performance misses the

fundamental unit of analysis. The primary carriers of intelligence are

institutions---the semantic niches in which agents live, learn, and act.

Future work may sharpen the derived-geometric aspects, integrate explicit RSVP

dynamics, and apply the resulting machinery to concrete questions in governance,

alignment, and institutional design.
